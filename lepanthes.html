01 / Why Lepanthes

Micro-endemics on a single land-use decision

Lepanthes is one of the most species-rich orchid genera of the Neotropics, and one of the hardest to see. The plants grow on mossy trunks, shaded branches and wet ravine walls, and their flowers are smaller than a grain of rice.

The cloud forests on the foothills of Cofre de Perote have lost an estimated 90% of their original extent to agriculture, real estate, charcoal production and fire. Several Lepanthes from this region were described as new to science in 2025 and are known from a single site. Our surveys have found further localities that may hold additional, still undescribed taxa.

For organisms like these, a single clearing, a fire or one change in land use can remove a large share of a species' global population. None of our target taxa had been assessed for the IUCN Red List.

04 / How we work

One plant, one complete record

Every record is built around a single documented plant. The photograph, the herbarium voucher, the georeference and the leaf tissue preserved in silica gel all come from the same individual, so that identification, genetics and distribution data can always be traced back to one specimen.

Surveys follow belt transects laid along elevational gradients through remnant forest. For each plant we record GPS position, host tree, height on the trunk and canopy openness with a spherical densiometer, in both wet and dry seasons so that flowering plants can be identified.

Distribution data feed IUCN Red List assessments, with Extent of Occurrence calculated by minimum convex polygon and Area of Occupancy on 2 × 2 km grid cells. Threats are documented at every site through direct observation and interviews with ejido landholders.

05 / Possible new species

Careful before a name

Some of the plants we have documented do not match any described species. A suspected new species is only a hypothesis until it has been studied in flower, preserved, illustrated on a botanical plate and reviewed by specialists in the genus. We follow that sequence before any nomenclatural decision, and we share material with the researchers working on Mexican Lepanthes.

Until then these plants appear on this site without names, and their localities stay private.

06 / Partners

A Mexican contribution to a continental picture

Tissue from vouchered plants contributes to a Latin American phylogenomic project on Lepanthes led by Dr Tatiana Arias (Orchids for Peace, Universidad de las Américas), which builds on published plastome phylogenomics (Arias et al. 2025, BMC Ecology and Evolution 25: 79). Our role is to make sure the Mexican material enters that work well identified and fully documented.

Herbario AMO, which holds most of the historical Lepanthes material from México, receives our vouchers. Assessments are prepared through the IUCN SSC Orchid Specialist Group, and our results are shared in Spanish with the Universidad Veracruzana and local authorities to inform site protection.

Fieldwork in the Cofre de Perote cloud forests is supported by a Rufford Small Grant through the project El Oro del Cofre.
