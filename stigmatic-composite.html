01 / The material

A cavity filled with a cellular composite

In the epidendroid orchids studied here, pollinia enter a concave stigma filled with secretion. This material has to accommodate the pollen mass and retain it as the pollinator withdraws, then support the longer interval of pollen hydration and tube growth.

Fresh microscopy reveals a continuous extracellular matrix carrying eleutherocytes: detached, non-pollen cells of the stigmatic secretion. Oscillatory measurements reveal a network that stores elastic stress and dissipates energy, with a broad distribution of relaxation times.

How we measured it

Rheology
Amplitude and frequency sweeps, creep–recovery, and first-heating temperature ramps on an Anton Paar MCR 302e.
Microscopy
Fresh, unstained polarized-light images, stage-micrometer calibration, projected cell area, and verified cell dimensions.
Sampling
Nine rheology pools: two each for four genera, one for Laelia. Eight pools supplied analysed frequency spectra. Microscopy and gravimetry used separate specimens.
02 / Compare the measured materials

One continuum of stiffness and relaxation

The genus means follow the order Cattleya → Laelia → Phalaenopsis → Cymbidium → Dendrobium: stiffer matrices are less frequency-sensitive and relax more slowly. The intermediate genera overlap in plateau stiffness.

03 / Cells and matrix

A crowded extracellular architecture

Detached cells were observed in all nine microscopy genera: Cattleya, Laelia, Phalaenopsis, Cymbidium, Dendrobium, Oncidium, Vanda, Coelogyne, and Masdevallia. Their appearance ranges from near-isodiametric to fusiform and filamentous.

Projected cell area fractions are approximately 31–57% in fields with limited overlap. These two-dimensional measurements describe cellular loading in mounted images; they are not cell volume fractions. The Phalaenopsis estimate of 90 ± 4% is an overlap-limited upper bound. A separate-session field gave 43%.

What the microscopy establishes

A cellular phase embedded in a continuous matrix. Viability was not assayed here, and mechanical reinforcement by the cells has not been demonstrated. Aspect ratios were available for only two of the rheology genera, so shape–rheology correlations were not evaluated.

04 / Deformation

Stiffness and strain tolerance are different properties

Every amplitude sweep has a low-strain plateau followed by strain softening. The linear viscoelastic limit is defined consistently by a 5% drop in G′. In Cattleya, this occurs at only 0.026–0.11% strain; the stiffest material is also the most strain-fragile.

The G′–G″ crossover in an amplitude sweep is a separate measure. Cattleya becomes loss-dominated at 0.08–0.39% strain, whereas Laelia and Phalaenopsis sustain about 23–42%. Critical stress varies from 21 to 610 Pa, a smaller range than plateau modulus.

05 / Frequency and time

A broad spectrum of relaxation times

Over 1–100 rad s⁻¹, G′ follows effective power laws with exponents n′ = 0.12–0.71; the corresponding G″ exponents are 0.01–0.45. The exponents change across the spectrum, and n′ exceeds n″ in every pool in this common window. The response therefore does not reduce to one frequency-independent exponent or one relaxation time.

No genus reached terminal flow within its measured window: down to 0.005 rad s⁻¹ in the longest sweeps, and 0.026 rad s⁻¹ for Dendrobium. A crossover marks the balance of storage and loss within a broad spectrum; it is not evidence that terminal relaxation has been completed.

Among the five crossing spectra, ωc spans 0.00862–19 rad s⁻¹ while Gc spans 2.93–11.9 kPa. Rescaling by these quantities brings the spectra close together within about one decade of the crossover. This is a local superposition; the curves diverge farther away, and Cattleya cannot be scaled by an unobserved crossover.

This pattern suggests that association lifetimes may differ much more than the density of load-bearing constraints. That is a physical interpretation to test through composition and perturbation experiments, rather than a measured molecular mechanism.

06 / Structure and mechanics

Cellular loading is a candidate variable

Projected cellular loading follows the rheological ordering more closely than gravimetric water content. Across five genera, its rank association with stiffness is ρ = +0.6, and with n′ is ρ = −0.6. Both strengthen in magnitude to 0.8 when the overlap-limited Phalaenopsis result is excluded or replaced.

The complete five-genus associations have exact p = 0.35; none of the structure–rheology comparisons reached a small permutation p value. The measurements come from different specimens matched at genus level. They motivate experiments on crowding, without establishing a causal cellular contribution.

The next discriminating experiment

Gently deplete and reconstitute cells in the same genus at fixed matrix, with sham-processed controls. If cells mainly slow relaxation, the prediction is a shift in ωc at nearly constant Gc. Matched microscopy, quantitative chemistry, and cell-viability measurements would help separate crowding from matrix composition.

07 / Supporting results

Creep and thermal response

Creep within the linear regime

Applied stresses of 5–20 Pa were below the critical stress of every pool. Stress-independent compliance in Laelia and Cymbidium S2 supports a linear response.

At 10 Pa, recovery within 264 s was approximately 61% in Cymbidium S2, 23% in Phalaenopsis S2, and 16% in Dendrobium S2. Incomplete recovery over this finite window reflects slow and partly viscous relaxation; it does not establish yielding.

Recovery in Cattleya and Laelia could not be quantified reliably because of strain drift and baseline offsets, respectively.

Moderate thermal softening

During first heating from 10 to 30 °C, G′ decreased by 15–39% in the pools tested. Tan δ changed by at most 0.09 and did not cross unity.

Heating to 50 °C caused irreversible changes consistent with water loss. Post-cycle data therefore cannot establish reversible thermal behaviour. Analysis focuses on the first heating segment up to 35 °C.

Time–temperature superposition was not tested. The dehydration control is one repeat sweep on a Phalaenopsis S2 load.

08 / Biological and materials context

From matrix relaxation to pollen retention

A matrix that responds elastically during a brief load and relaxes over longer times could resist displacement during pollinator withdrawal while gradually conforming around deposited pollinia. Separate Cattleya walkeriana withdrawal experiments are consistent with this possibility: faster withdrawal shifted failure to the caudicle–pollinium junctions and left pollinia in the stigma.

Bulk oscillatory rheology does not measure insertion, pull-off, or interfacial adhesion directly. Intact-flower tests are needed to connect the spectra to retention quantitatively. Relaxation over hours or weeks remains a prediction beyond the experimental frequency window.

The cell-laden stigmatic matrix also provides a natural comparison with biological gels, biofilms, and engineered hydrogels. It offers a series of related plant extracellular materials for testing how matrix associations and cellular packing affect stress relaxation. For Orchidarc, documenting these systems connects orchid diversity to experimental materials research.

09 / Evidence and limits

What this study can establish

Measured

  • Eleutherocytes in a continuous matrix in every microscopy genus sampled.
  • Linear viscoelastic ranges and broad power-law frequency spectra in five genera.
  • Common ordering of stiffness, frequency exponents, loss tangent, and crossover behaviour at genus level.
  • Limited-window creep recovery and first-heating softening.

Still to resolve

  • Cellular crowding versus matrix chemistry as the source of the continuum.
  • Cell viability, cell–matrix coupling, and post-pollination changes.
  • Wall slip, pre-shear history, and longer-time relaxation.
  • Quantitative links between bulk rheology and intact-flower pollen retention.

One or two pools per genus, mostly horticultural hybrids, limit taxon-level and evolutionary inference. Smooth plates, one gap per load, a fixed test sequence, and unlogged enclosure humidity leave experimental effects to test. Projected cell area is not a three-dimensional fraction. The materials comparison is not evidence of genus-specific pollinator adaptation.

Supporting composition and microscopy qualifications

Qualitative phytochemical assays, freeze-dried SEM, and thermogravimetry are supporting analyses on separately sourced Ecuadorian hybrids. They do not establish quantitative molecular composition of the rheology pools. Freeze-dried SEM shows a film-forming residue rather than native hydrated architecture.
