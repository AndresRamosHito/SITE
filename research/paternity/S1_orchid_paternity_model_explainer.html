Companion to Orchid paternity: pollinaria, single-sire fruits and pollination-dependent genetic funnels

The orchid paternity model

A stochastic, individual-based simulation of diploid adults through 50 discrete, non-overlapping generations, without clonal growth, dormancy or a seed bank. Every scenario is neutral and cross-only: the mother is excluded from her own donor pool, so the model describes outcrossing, as in self-incompatible orchids.

Paternity regimes are specified distributions, not fitted species or evolving traits: single-sire fractions are set by the regime rather than emerging from it, and pollen transport is not simulated.

Engine pollinaria_model_v3_3.py, release v3.3.0 (params_v3_3_final.json), with the 2026 pollination-opportunity, factorial and establishment-correlation runs.

The hard-pollinium evidence comes from three species and 477 capsule progeny arrays, only 18 genotyped with microsatellites or SNPs. With ten seedlings per capsule, a sire contributing 10% of the seeds is missed in about one capsule in three (0.910 ≈ 0.35): the data exclude frequent multiple paternity with a substantial second sire, but not rare multiply sired capsules or a second sire with a small share.

Ae = 1Σi p2i

pi is the share of distinct father i. Shares of a repeated resident are merged first; each external candidate is its own father.

(⅓, ⅓, ⅓)→ Ae = 3.00 (0.8, 0.1, 0.1)→ Ae = 1.52

Three fathers each, yet very different concentration.

π = Σi p2i = 1/Ae

Within-fruit paternal identity: the probability that two seeds of the fruit share a father. Averaged across fruits, π̄ is the model analogue of the correlated paternity rp estimated from progeny arrays, and 1/π̄ corresponds to the effective number of pollen donors (Smouse and Robledo-Arnuncio 2005).

Aₑ is assigned before recruitment, so a small paternal share may leave no retained offspring at all. Aₑ and single-sire fractions average generations 1–50 within replicates; π̄ uses fruits sampled every fifth generation (up to 400 per replicate).

Bf ~ Bernoulli(q)

One gate per fruit couples all its siblings: a fruit either can recruit or it cannot. The gate represents the shared fate of a capsule’s seeds, which are released together, disperse mostly within a few metres of the mother and depend on patchily distributed mycorrhizal fungi and microsites.

Rf | Bf ~ Poisson(Bf μ), μ = Sε = 10

S is a nominal seed count; the code never creates S individual seeds.

E[Rf]= qμ = 5 Var(Rf)= qμ + q(1 − q)μ2 = 30 P(Rf = 0)= 1 − q + qe−μ ≈ 0.50002

Analytical consequences of the implemented distribution, before density regulation: φLfqSε = 5.25 potential recruits per adult. In the code the gate variable is named compatible; it is a recruitment gate, not mating compatibility. The gate is the limiting case of complete within-fruit correlation in establishment; relaxes it.

Each generation-zero adult is one founder carrying a unique pair of marker alleles, so a population at K = 500 starts with 500 founders and 1,000 alleles. Each external donor and each immigrant brings two new alleles.

Sampling is without replacement. Otherwise every candidate remains.

Previous adults do not survive. With about 2,625 potential recruits for 500 places, most assigned offspring never become adults. From here on, a recruit is an offspring retained through establishment and density regulation into the next adult generation.

wi= retained recruits sired by resident i CV2m= Var(w)w̄2, over all N residents Me= N1 + CV2m = 1Σi c2i

ci = wi/Σw, resident i’s share of resident-sired recruits. N counts all resident adults at the start of the generation, including those that did not flower or sire (ddof = 0). External fathers and immigrants are excluded.

CV²ₘ is the opportunity for selection through male genetic contribution (Arnold and Wade 1984); because w is counted after establishment and density regulation, it includes the filtering of whole sibships as well as siring success. Mₑ, the effective number of resident male contributors, is the male component of drift, not population Nₑ, which also includes maternal variance and the male–female covariance (Crow and Denniston 1988; Caballero 1994). The two express the same distribution, so they are not independent evidence. The effective number of mothers, Fₑ, is defined in the same way from each resident mother’s retained recruits.

CV2m ≈ 1 + 1/aφ + r̄ π̄

Partitioning each father’s recruits among fruits, in a closed population at carrying capacity (Supporting Methods).

donor access = (1 + 1/a)/φ = 3.81

Inequality generated by flowering and by gamma donor weights (a = 0.6, φ = 0.7). Mating among fruits, a donor siring capsules on several plants, is captured here and is present in every regime.

sibling packaging = r̄ π̄, r̄ ≈ 1/(φLfq) = 1.91

r̄ is the mean number of retained recruits per establishing fruit. Packaging was 1.91 under strict paternity and 0.73 under granular pollen: the regimes differ only in this term.

ΔCV2m ≈ (π̄1 − π̄2)/(φLfq)

Flowering, flower number, fruit set and the gate act only through their product φLfq, that is, through sibship size, while recruitment is limited by density rather than seed supply. The prediction matched the all-generation mean CV²ₘ within 1.1% in every regime. The milkweed comparison, with fewer and larger sibships (r̄ = 4.37), had the highest skew.

H = 1 − Σj q2j

qj is the frequency of marker allele j among retained adults. H is founder-allele diversity, the gene diversity of the simulated neutral marker, not observed heterozygosity.

H0 = 1 − 12K = 0.999

NeH ≈ −1/(2β)

In closed populations without mutation H decays by a factor of 1 − 1/(2Nₑ) per generation, so β, the slope of ln H̄ on generation over generations 10–50, gives the inbreeding effective population size, by the large-Nₑ approximation (within 0.5 of the exact inversion at the reported values). External input raises H towards 1 and compresses absolute differences, so homozygosity, 1 − H, is also reported as the relative measure of drift.

retention = #{ identities < K still present }

Founder retention counts generation-zero founders whose marker ancestry persists; identity richness also counts identities introduced later and can stay high while original founders disappear.

Summary convention: Aₑ and single-sire fractions average generations 1–50 within replicates; π̄ uses fruits sampled every fifth generation; NeH uses generations 10–50; everything else describes generation 50. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals of replicate means (1,000 resamples); contrasts between regimes resample each regime’s replicates independently.

Demography and recruitment are held constant; only the paternity regime differs. K = 500, no external pollen or immigration, 100 populations per regime.

Concentrating paternity concentrates siblings among fathers, and their contributions vary more after recruitment.

Strict paternity raised CV²ₘ from 4.54 to 5.60 and lowered Mₑ from 90.9 to 76.6, a reduction of 15.7% [13.6, 17.9]. The four regimes form a series that is monotonic in π̄.

H was 0.856 under strict paternity and 0.875 under granular pollen: a difference of 0.0191 [0.0093, 0.0294], or 15.2% [7.3, 24.3] more homozygosity.

NeH fell from 187 to 160, a 14.7% [5.5, 22.8] reduction that matches the 15.7% reduction in Mₑ. Strict paternity also retained fewer original founders: 13.08 against 14.96.

Mean Mₑ is computed per replicate and then averaged, so it need not equal 500 / (1 + mean CV²ₘ). Bootstrap intervals describe simulation uncertainty under chosen parameters, not uncertainty across orchid taxa.

ε = 2.5 × 10−6/f, so φLfqSε = 5.25

Strict, low-sire and granular paternity crossed with fruit set from 0.025 to 0.50 (K = 500, closed). Potential recruits per adult stay constant: the gradient changes how a fixed pool is divided among fruits, not total recruitment.

r̄: 0.95 → 19.4 recruits per establishing fruit

Mₑ fell in every regime, most steeply under single paternity: from 87.5 to 21.1 under strict paternity, but only from 96.5 to 42.0 under granular pollen. Dashed lines: Mₑ = K/[1 + (1 + 1/a)/φ + π̄/(φLfq)].

The strict-paternity reduction in Mₑ rose from 9.3% [6.9, 11.6] to 49.8% [47.2, 52.4], following the analytical expectation (all-generation CV²ₘ within 1.2% in all 15 cells). Drift followed, less steeply and with wider intervals: the NeH reduction was 9.4% at f = 0.50 and 27.6% at f = 0.025, and its interval included zero at f = 0.10.

The effective number of mothers, which does not depend on paternity, fell from 147–150 to 23. At f = 0.025 the effective number of fathers under strict paternity (21.1) had converged on it, because each fruit then contributes one mother and one father.

The f = 0.25 cells repeat the closed-reference conditions with independent seeds: their 18.5% reduction differs from 15.7% by 2.9 percentage points [−0.3, 5.8], within replicate-set variation.

u ~ Beta, mean q, variance ρq(1 − q)

The gate is replaced by a fruit-level establishment multiplier. ρ = 1 recovers the gate exactly, ρ = 0 gives every fruit the mean multiplier q, so that establishment is independent among seeds, and 0.25 and 0.5 give partial correlation (K = 500, closed).

packaging = π̄[q + ρ(1 − q)]/(φLfq)

Relaxing the gate shrinks sibling packaging by the predicted factor; the generalised decomposition predicted all-generation CV²ₘ within 2.2% in all 16 cells.

At f = 0.25 the Mₑ reduction fell from 18.5% with the gate to 9.0% [6.7, 11.4] with independent establishment, and the reductions in NeH (3.4%) and homozygosity (3.9%) were no longer distinguishable from zero.

At f = 0.025 the Mₑ reduction stayed at 44–50% and the NeH reduction at 22–28% at every level of correlation, all intervals excluding zero. Shared establishment sets the size of the diversity effect at high fruit set, but not the existence of the funnel at low fruit set.

ΔCV2m ≈ Δπ̄ · r̄eff, r̄eff = r̄[q + ρ(1 − q)]

Across the five fruit sets, the closed reference and the six correlation cells, the strict-minus-granular difference in all-generation CV²ₘ followed this line (slope 1.006; R² 0.9998; largest deviation 2.2%; Figure S2): fruit set, establishment and its correlation act on the paternity effect only through sibship size.

Mₑ and H: granular → strict, generation-50 means (Table S3)

In all eight open settings strict paternity reduced Mₑ, by 15.5–27.1%, and every interval excluded zero (closed reference: 15.7%). The four settings form a factorial that separates external input from donor evenness.

Donor evenness drives the larger reductions: with even weights (a = 3) they were 25.2–27.1%, with or without input, because even weights shrink the donor-access term and leave sibling packaging a larger share of male skew. Input alone changed the reduction by less than five percentage points.

Input compresses the diversity difference: ΔH fell from 0.0259 and 0.0116 under lower input to 0.0033 and 0.0009 with input only (K = 150 and 500), whereas even donors alone left it large (0.0759 and 0.0081). Input replaced original ancestry: fewer than 0.15 founders remained, and H approached its ceiling.

Homozygosity stayed higher under strict paternity in every setting, by 9.0–34.2%; only at K = 150 with lower input did the intervals for ΔH and homozygosity include zero. External input compresses the diversity difference; every external contribution brings new alleles, so the external source is effectively unlimited, and absolute differences under high input should be read in that light.

  1. Pollinator decline. Pollinator decline or fragmentation that reduces fruit set should reduce effective size more strongly in hard-pollinium orchids than in species with mixed paternity, even when recruitment is maintained.
  2. Deceptive versus rewarding. Within clades and habitats, and after accounting for longevity, clonality and census structure, deceptive species with low fruit set should have lower effective size relative to census size than rewarding relatives with high fruit set.
  3. Pleurothallids. Lepanthes and Dracula, which combine pollinia transferred whole with near-universal self-incompatibility and very low fruit set, should have the deepest funnels. Demographic estimates of effective size in Lepanthes are already small (Tremblay and Ackerman 2001), and progeny arrays from pleurothallid capsules would test the prediction directly.
  4. A direct test. Genotyping natural recruitment cohorts around mapped mothers would show how often recruits are full siblings from one capsule, and so estimate sibship size, the parameter on which the funnel depends.

These are tests of effective size, not of diversity in single populations. In the closed reference the expected difference in H (0.019) was about half the standard deviation of H among replicate populations (0.035–0.039), so the funnel is detected by estimating Nₑ or by comparing many populations, not by comparing H between two sites.

Because π̄ is equivalent to correlated paternity, the depth of the funnel can be measured directly from progeny arrays across pollen-unit types (Dorken and Perry 2017).

Seed number sets persistence, not the funnel. Under strict paternity (K = 500, f = 0.12, lower input), no population persisted at 250,000 seeds or fewer and all persisted at 500,000 or more, bracketing the replacement threshold φLfqSε = 1 at S ≈ 397,000. Above it, CV²ₘ and founder retention did not change with seed number (Figure S1). If pollination failure pushes recruitment below replacement, populations decline, and the constraint is demographic before it is genetic.

Every sibling of a single-sire fruit shares one father, so the number of fruits that recruit a generation sets the scale of its effective paternal pool.

Pollination opportunity controls, through sibship size, a genetic funnel whose depth is predicted by correlated paternity. The model does not estimate effective size in real orchid populations, and a link to orchid diversification through selection on pre-pollination placement is a hypothesis it does not test.

  • Outcrossing only. Selfing deepens the funnel, so the results apply directly to self-incompatible orchids and are a lower bound for self-compatible ones.
  • One recruitment episode. No adult survival, clonal growth, dormancy, or seed and protocorm banks. Across a lifetime the reductions are diluted if siring success varies randomly among episodes, and retained if it is repeatable.
  • Shared establishment. The gate is the limiting case of shared seed fate, and co-recruitment of siblings from one fruit has not been measured directly. Without it the effect halves at f = 0.25 but the funnel persists at low fruit set.
  • Not simulated. Pollen transport, pollinium number, partial deposition, stigma receptivity and floral longevity. Legacy carryover and pollen_flow_distance fields do not drive transport; x, m and a do.

diploid: two gene copies per individual

One copy comes from each parent. The variants of a gene are its alleles, and the pair an individual carries at one locus (one position in the genome) is its genotype.

homozygous:two copies of one allele, AA or BB heterozygous:two different alleles, AB or AC

Heterozygosity is a basic measure of genetic variation: the more individuals carry two different alleles, the more variation the population holds.

Ho = heterozygous plantsplants genotyped = 5⁄8 = 0.625

Observed heterozygosity is counted directly from genotypes, one locus at a time, then averaged over loci.

In real populations it is measured by genotyping a sample of plants at neutral markers, such as microsatellites or single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which are assumed not to affect fitness. The model does not report observed heterozygosity; its H is the expected form on the next primer page.

pA = 6⁄16, pB = 5⁄16, pC = 4⁄16, pD = 1⁄16

Pool the 16 gene copies and count each allele.

He = 1 − Σi p2i

Σpi2 is the chance that two gene copies drawn at random, with replacement, are the same allele (the coloured squares); He is the chance they differ, the heterozygosity expected under Hardy–Weinberg proportions.

He= 1 − 0.305 = 0.695 corrected= 16⁄15 × 0.695 = 0.742

Here expected exceeds the observed 0.625. Small samples bias the plain formula low, so field studies apply Nei’s (1978) correction, 2n/(2n − 1) for n plants. Observed well below expected is commonly read as a sign of inbreeding or population structure.

founders: H0 = 1 − 1/(2K) = 0.999

The model’s H is this expected form, with allele frequencies qj among retained adults (). Every founder allele is unique, so H starts near its ceiling of 1; alleles are lost by chance each generation (genetic drift), so it tends to fall, while immigrants add new ones. All draws come from a seeded pseudo-random generator: the same seed reproduces the same run exactly, which is why asks for scenario order to be preserved.

effective number = 1Σi p2i

The number of equal contributors that would give the same concentration. Three equal fathers give 3; shares of 0.8, 0.1 and 0.1 give 1.52, much closer to a single father ().

CV2m = Var(w)w̄2, Me = N1 + CV2m

CV²ₘ measures how unequal male success is, relative to the average. Mₑ is the number of equally successful residents that would be just as unequal. It describes resident males in one generation; it is not Nₑ, the effective size of the whole population ().

Each replicate population is an independent run with the same inputs; results are means over 100 of them. A percentile bootstrap resamples those populations with replacement, 1,000 times, and reads the 95% interval from percentiles of the resampled means. It measures simulation uncertainty, not variation among orchid taxa (scenes and ).

When the interval for a difference includes zero, the runs do not distinguish that difference from no difference (). An axis that starts above zero magnifies small gaps, so it is always labelled ().
