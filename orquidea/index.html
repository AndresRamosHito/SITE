OrquIDea — Citizen Science for Wild Orchids

Citizen science for wild orchids.

A field app by Orchidarc for documenting, protecting, and learning from wild orchid observations.

Open OrquIDea Learn why locations are protected
Observation only. OrquIDea must never be used to locate, collect, remove, trade, or disturb wild orchids. Sensitive species locations are protected.
§ 01

What is OrquIDea?

OrquIDea is Orchidarc’s citizen-science platform for recording wild orchid observations in Mexico. The app allows users to submit photographs, dates, general locality information, habitat notes, and suggested identifications. Observations can then be discussed and reviewed by the community and Orchidarc collaborators.

The first deployment focuses on orchids of the Sierra de Oaxaca. Future versions may support additional Orchidarc projects, including Veracruz cloud forests, reintroduction monitoring, orchid-pollinator interactions, and conservation fieldwork.

§ 02

How it works.

OrquIDea is designed for responsible field documentation, not extraction or site disclosure.

01 — Photograph

Photograph

Users photograph wild orchids in the field. The app removes EXIF metadata before upload.

02 — Record

Record

Users add observation date, general locality, habitat notes, and a suggested identification if known.

03 — Protect

Protect

Sensitive species locations are hidden, fuzzed, or generalized to reduce the risk of illegal collection.

04 — Verify

Verify

The community and Orchidarc collaborators can discuss, suggest IDs, and help verify observations.

§ 03

Why exact locations are protected.

Wild orchids are vulnerable to habitat loss, illegal extraction, and trade. For this reason, OrquIDea does not publicly expose exact locations for sensitive species. Public maps and feeds show protected, generalized, or fuzzed locality information. This allows observations to support conservation without putting populations at risk.

Photos are handled separately from GPS records. Public outputs should use only controlled, masked, or generalized data views.

§ 04

What your observations support.

Responsible records help Orchidarc and collaborators understand orchid populations without exposing sensitive sites.

  • orchid distribution records;
  • flowering-time observations;
  • habitat and host-tree notes;
  • conservation monitoring;
  • reintroduction follow-up;
  • community education;
  • Orchidarc research and field planning.
§ 05

Field rules: observe, never collect.

When using OrquIDea, follow these rules:

  • Do not collect wild orchids.
  • Do not damage flowers, roots, bark, host trees, or surrounding habitat.
  • Do not publish exact locations of rare orchids.
  • Stay on existing paths when possible.
  • Respect local communities, landowners, and protected areas.
  • Report illegal trade or extraction through the app or Orchidarc contact channels.
