01 / The maternal interface

The cells with which a flower receives its pollen

A pollinarium packages the pollen that leaves a flower. The receiving stigma supplies the maternal side of that encounter: capture, retention, hydration, and the environment through which pollen tubes grow.

In the sampled epidendroids, the removable stigmatic composite contains a continuous extracellular matrix and detached cells called eleutherocytes. Their recurring architecture, cellular diversity, and interaction with pollen make this maternal interface a research subject in its own right.

The manuscript proposes that the composite functions as a maternal accessory organ: a separable cellular material acting on behalf of the flower. This is a functional interpretation that draws on the microscopy and companion mechanics studies.

What counts as an eleutherocyte?

A detached, non-pollen cellular profile with a continuous outline and internal contents that remain coherent through focus.

Included
Bounded cellular profiles in fresh, unstained composite.
Excluded
Pollen, pollen tubes, attached tissue fragments, broken outlines, fungal hyphae, and yeast-like profiles.
Scope of the definition
Morphological: it does not establish tissue of origin, organelle identity, or viability.

03 / Comparison and timing

Occurrence depends on what and when we sample

Observation

Sarcoglottis

A male-phase S. sceptrodes flower contained abundant eleutherocyte-like cells. This orchidoid comparison does not establish their developmental origin or equivalence to the epidendroid cells.

Unresolved

Vanilla

The V. planifolia preparation had sparse ambiguous profiles, beaded strands, and needle-shaped crystals. Cell presence could not be scored. It is not an absence record.

Extraction result

Slipper orchids

No comparable removable composite was recovered from three Paphiopedilum hybrid flowers or two Phragmipedium longifolium flowers. Extraction failure does not establish anatomical absence.

One flower, two observations: Mormodes

The M. fractiflexa stigma was initially dry, with a twisted column and no visible or recoverable composite. A cell-containing layer was present 48 h after manual pollinarium removal. This fits a transition towards the female phase; an untreated comparison is needed to test whether removal accelerated it.

04 / Pollen tubes and live preparations

Growth among maternal cells

In self-pollinated Coelogyne pulverula at 48 h, pollen tubes ran between and around detached cells. In one bright-field field, a filament could be traced continuously to a pollen tetrad, providing direct evidence of its identity. Comparable filaments were not seen in the 48 h unpollinated flower.

The 24 h self-pollinated C. pulverula flower had enlarged tetrads without a traceable tube. Coelogyne Burfordiense at 24 h had angular tetrads and a few short tube-like profiles. These preparations came from different flowers, so they do not form a replicated developmental time course.

Movement in eleven recordings

Intracellular material moved within stable cell outlines in all eleven live recordings: seven from Sobralia and four from Coelogyne. The Coelogyne recordings also showed movement of cells and matrix-associated particles.

This is consistent with living cytoplasm. It does not replace a viability assay, and movement alone does not assign organelles or prove a cellular function.

How the material was documented

Fresh, unstained mounts were examined within about one minute of extraction at Jardín Botánico Lankester in July 2026. Still images used Leica bright-field microscopy; recordings were filmed from the live microscope monitor.

One flower supplied each accession outside the Coelogyne series. Within that series, one flower supplied each treatment and time point.

05 / Proposed functions

What might the detached cells do?

These are hypotheses developed in the manuscript. Observing cells and pollen tubes in the same material does not by itself demonstrate nutrition, guidance, recognition, or DNA uptake.

Hypothesis 01

Nourish

Released starch, sugars, or other contents could support pollen-tube growth.

Test

Track cell contents and labelled material before and after tube passage.

Hypothesis 02

Guide

Cell packing and matrix mechanics could influence the paths taken by growing tubes.

Test

Change cellular loading while controlling the matrix and measure tube trajectories.

Hypothesis 03

Recognise

Cell state or signalling could participate in compatibility responses.

Test

Compare replicated self- and cross-pollinations in a self-incompatible species.

Tri-individual stigma · A proposed scenario

One recipient. Three possible plant identities.

Pollen could arrive together with stigmatic composite retained from an earlier flower. If that earlier flower belongs to a third plant, the recipient could contain cells from two maternal sources alongside the pollen donor.

A · Pollen donor

Produces the pollen being transported.

B · Earlier stigma

Could contribute cells during a previous visit.

C · Recipient

Receives pollen into its own cellular matrix.

The proposed recipient contains

A · pollenB · carried cellsC · recipient cells

Natural transfer of third-plant cells and DNA exposure have not been established by this survey. DNA exposure would not demonstrate DNA integration or inheritance. The first test is to detect third-plant markers in composite recovered from naturally pollinated stigmas.

06 / Evidence and next tests

A comparative starting point for orchid maternity

Established in this survey

  • Detached, bounded cells in every sampled epidendroid genus.
  • Variation in cell outlines, visible contents, and packing.
  • Intracellular movement in eleven live recordings.
  • Pollen tubes among detached cells in a self-pollinated C. pulverula flower at 48 h.

Research programme

  • Broader sampling, with several flowers of known age per species.
  • Sections through development to establish cellular origin and detachment.
  • Viability, DNA, and histochemical assays.
  • Replicated, bagged pollinations and tests of cell transfer and retention.

The survey is qualitative. Flowers were not bagged; age after anthesis was not recorded. Fields are technical observations chosen to document variation, and fresh extraction cannot establish in-situ developmental detachment by itself. Bright-field appearance does not identify organelles.

The evolutionary interpretation

The manuscript proposes that the detached-cell composite is widespread and may have arisen early in Epidendroideae. Its occurrence in Sobralia, alongside published evidence from Cephalanthera, motivates this hypothesis. Unsampled lineages, published absences in some Neottieae, and possible independent origins remain important. Twenty positive sampled genera do not establish universality across orchids.
