01 / Pollination mechanicsSubmitted · BJLS

When pollen stays behind.

A slow pull can carry pollen back out of a flower. A faster pull can leave it in the stigma. Follow the two failure pathways in Cattleya walkeriana, with synchronized force and video.

02 / Soft materialsSubmitted · ACS JPCB

A living matrix that yields and holds.

The receiving stigma is a cellular soft material. Across five orchid genera, rheology reveals a continuum of stiffness and relaxation. Explore the measurements in our 13 October submission to The Journal of Physical Chemistry B.

Rheology · microscopy · power-law viscoelasticityExplore the stigmatic matrix ↗
03 / Reproductive anatomyManuscript in preparation

The cells at the heart of orchid maternity.

Detached maternal cells recur in the stigmatic composites of twenty genera. Browse the microscopy atlas, follow pollen tubes among the cells, and see which proposed functions remain to be tested.

20 genera · 25 accessions · fresh microscopyMeet the eleutherocytes ↗
04 / Reproductive adhesivesManuscript under revision

How an orchid catches a ride.

Orchid pollen travels on an adhesive connection. Compare the chemistry, structure and mechanical behaviour of reproductive adhesives across genera, from lipid-rich to polysaccharide-rich materials.

FTIR-ATR · anatomy · adhesion testingExplore orchid adhesives ↗
05 / Reproductive evolutionEvidence synthesis & modelling

Many seeds. How many fathers?

Pollinaria package pollen together, but what does that mean for the next generation? Connect evidence on within-fruit paternity with a population model of male contribution and conditional changes in genetic diversity.
