About Orchidarc · Mexico

We study Mexico's orchids where they grow.

Orchidarc is a UK-registered charity working mainly in Veracruz, Mexico. We document wild orchid populations, study their biology, and develop conservation work around the plants and places we know.

Most of our work starts with a field notebook: an orchid, the tree or rock it grows on, the forest around it, and a record of what has changed since the last visit.

We work in the cloud forests of Veracruz and other orchid habitats in Mexico. At the 14-hectare Orchidarc Reserve in Ixhuacán de los Reyes, we monitor orchids and their habitat while developing practical conservation and cultivation facilities. Beyond the reserve, field projects follow particular species and populations, not just attractive flowers.

Field observations lead to identification, microscopy, population studies and, where appropriate and permitted, genetic sampling. The results also tell us what we still do not know. That matters when deciding which conservation actions are justified.

Field practice

How we work.

There is no single technique that conserves an orchid. Our projects combine field observation, follow-up visits, careful cultivation, and research with the people who know the sites.

01 · Field records

Find, document and return.

We photograph plants in their habitats, record flowering and ecological context, compare identifications, and revisit populations where possible. Our digital herbarium makes selected records available without turning sensitive locations into a collecting guide.

02 · Habitat and cultivation

Work on the conditions the orchids need.

At the Veracruz reserve, our priorities include monitoring forest conditions, restoring suitable host-tree habitat, and rebuilding cultivation infrastructure. Symbiotic propagation and acclimatisation work, including work on Acineta barkeri, is being developed; proposed facilities and reintroductions are not presented as completed projects.

03 · Research and assessment

Put observations to the test.

Species documentation feeds taxonomic work and the evidence needed for conservation assessments, including IUCN Red List work. Our research also investigates how orchid pollinia attach, how stigmatic matrices behave, and how reproduction shapes the next generation. These projects involve scientific collaborators and are reported with their limitations.

04 · Records and films

Make the work visible without exposing the plants.

Our documentaries follow orchids, their habitats and the people connected to them. Lily of Allsaints and Oaks and Legacies are available to watch; Orchids of Mexico is in development. We are also developing OrquIDea to support responsible orchid observations and protect sensitive locality information.

Field and laboratory · Reproduction

What makes orchid reproduction work?

Experimental studies of pollinarium mechanics, stigmatic materials and detached cells, alongside modelling of pollen transfer and paternity. The research connects field biology with materials science.

People and collaboration

A small team, several disciplines.

Orchidarc is coordinated by its founder and director, Andrés Ramos, with members and field collaborators in Mexico. Local field support, site management, plant documentation, and scientific work are organised around individual projects rather than a large permanent staff.

Andrés is a member of the IUCN SSC Orchid Specialist Group and undertakes doctoral research at the University of Tokyo. Project-specific scientific work also involves colleagues at Universidad Veracruzana, Jardín Botánico Lankester at the University of Costa Rica, and other botanical and research institutions. Individual affiliations should not be confused with institutional endorsement of every Orchidarc activity.

For field collaboration, research records, or documentary screenings, write to andresr@orchidarc.org.
