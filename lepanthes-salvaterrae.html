Lepanthes salvaterrae

Lepanthes salvaterrae is a recently described Mexican micro-endemic from the cloud forests of central Veracruz, known from Rancho Salvaterra near Xico and formally described by Solano, J.R.Carral and Téllez in 2025.

Lepanthes salvaterrae is a minute, caespitose epiphyte described from cloud forest in central Veracruz. The species belongs to the highly diverse Pleurothallidinae and is part of the exceptionally rich Lepanthes flora of Rancho Salvaterra, one of the most important known localities for the genus in Veracruz.

Diagnosis

The species is most similar to Lepanthes gabriellae, but differs by a combination of vegetative and floral characters: the peduncle is shorter than the leaf, the leaves are orbicular to orbicular-elliptic rather than narrowly elliptic, the sepals are marginally glabrous rather than reddish-ciliate, and the upper and lower lobes of the petals differ in shape and size.

Description

The plant is a caespitose epiphyte, 3.2–6.4 cm tall including the inflorescence. The stems are 9.0–27.0 mm long and covered by lepanthiform sheaths. Leaves are fleshy, bright green, orbicular to orbicular-elliptic, 10.0–16.5 × 5.0–8.5 mm, rounded and shortly two-lobed at the apex.

The inflorescence is racemose and longer than the leaf, reaching up to 26.0 mm. Flowers are 7.0–11.2 mm tall and 2.0–3.7 mm wide. The sepals are cream to pale yellow and densely suffused with red-purple. The petals are yellowish orange, with the upper lobe purple along the inner margin; the lip, column and anther cap are purple.

Field character. In the field, the species is especially recognizable by the rounded leaf shape, the small red-purple and yellow flower, and its occurrence on humid cloud-forest trunks in the Salvaterra area.

Distribution and ecology

Lepanthes salvaterrae is currently known as a Mexican micro-endemic restricted to the southern Sierra Madre Oriental in Veracruz. All known localities are concentrated in a small area near Xico, although nearby municipalities may contain additional suitable habitat.

It grows as an epiphyte in cloud forest between 2000 and 2160 m elevation. Reported host trees include Quercus delgadoana, Liquidambar styraciflua, Vaccinium leucanthum and Quercus corrugata. Associated orchid species include Lepanthes acuminata, L. calopetala, L. moorei, L. papilionacea and L. totontepecensis.

Etymology

The name refers to Salvaterra, a privately owned forested ranch in Veracruz dedicated to cloud-forest conservation. The species was discovered there during floristic work in the reserve.

Conservation

The species has not yet been evaluated by the IUCN Red List or by the Mexican extinction-risk method used under SEMARNAT. Although several collections are known, they all come from the same locality, within a privately owned reserve that protects the habitat.

The surrounding region is under pressure from tourism infrastructure near Xico, conversion of forest into coffee plantations, and increasing urbanization. At the same time, the site has conservation-compatible activities, including nature tourism and organic coffee production. Rancho Salvaterra is now recognized as one of the richest known Lepanthes localities in Mexico.

Reference
