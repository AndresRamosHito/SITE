Lepanthes salvaterrae

Lepanthes salvaterrae is a recently described Mexican micro-endemic from the cloud forests of central Veracruz. Orchidarc documented additional populations during AOS-supported field expeditions in Summer 2025.

Lepanthes salvaterrae is a miniature epiphyte from the cloud forests of central Veracruz. It was formally described in 2025 by Solano, J.R.Carral and Téllez, and is one of the newest additions to the known orchid flora of Mexico.

Diagnosis

The species is most similar to Lepanthes gabriellae, but differs by a combination of vegetative and floral characters: the peduncle is shorter than the leaf, the leaves are orbicular to orbicular-elliptic rather than narrowly elliptic, the sepals are marginally glabrous, and the upper and lower lobes of the petals differ in shape and size.

Field recognition

In the field, Lepanthes salvaterrae is recognizable by its small size, rounded leaves, and red-purple and yellow flowers. Like many members of the genus, it can be easily overlooked unless the bark surface is examined closely.

Orchidarc field update. During Summer 2025 expeditions supported by the American Orchid Society, Orchidarc documented two additional populations of Lepanthes salvaterrae in central Veracruz. These records suggest that the species remains highly localized, but may be more widely distributed within suitable cloud-forest fragments than originally known.

Habitat and ecology

The species grows as an epiphyte in humid cloud forest. Its persistence likely depends on stable canopy cover, high humidity, host-tree continuity, and the moss-rich bark microhabitats that support many miniature Pleurothallidinae.

Conservation

Lepanthes salvaterrae has not yet been evaluated by the IUCN Red List or by the Mexican extinction-risk method used under SEMARNAT. Its known distribution remains narrow and tied to cloud-forest habitat, a vegetation type under pressure from land-use change, fragmentation, and local development.

The discovery of additional populations by Orchidarc in 2025 strengthens the case for continued field surveys. For miniature orchids like Lepanthes, conservation cannot rely only on known localities; it also requires protecting the humid forest structure that makes these microhabitats possible.

Reference

Solano, R., Licona, E., Carral, R. & Téllez-Baños, B. 2025. Two new species, nomenclatural notes and an updated checklist in Lepanthes (Orchidaceae, Pleurothallidinae) from Mexico. Phytotaxa 687(1): 21–64.
